- The controller menus work from the keyboard. - A `Game.retroactive health` setting, off by default, with a row on the Options' Prefs tab. In Morrowind each level up adds a tenth of Endurance to health at the Endurance the player has then, so players learn to raise Endurance first. With the setting on, the player's base health is always the health character creation gave, half of the starting Strength plus the starting Endurance, plus a tenth of the current Endurance for each level after the first. The starting Strength and Endurance are worked out from the race and the class, as character creation sets them, so saves don't keep them, and each is no more than the attribute is now. `MechanicsManager` works the health out again whenever base Strength, base Endurance or level changes, and when the setting is turned on. The rules are `MWMechanics::retroactiveHealth` and `MWMechanics::startingAttribute`, with Google Tests. The `retroactive-health` scenario checks a Dark Elf Acrobat at level 10 with Endurance 60: 99 health with the setting, and unchanged without it. A balance review found that counting the current Strength and Endurance in the level 1 part, as a first version did, gave more health than Morrowind ever could, and that players of similar mods complained about it.
- A `Game.keep level up progress` setting, off by default, with a row on the Options' Prefs tab. - A first prototype of quest markers, on the `quest-markers` branch, for GitHub issue 2. [design/quest-markers.md](design/quest-markers.md) has the plan, the analysis behind it, and a comparison with the mods that already mark quests.
  - **The analysis.** `MWGui::QuestTargets` works out each quest's ways forward from the content, in about 20 ms for the three official files. A way forward is a line of dialogue whose result script runs `Journal` or `SetJournalIndex`, or a script that does. Each way becomes things to do: talk to the speaker, kill an actor that a line needs dead, get an item that a line needs, or deal with the object a script is on. A line that needs someone who speaks in the quest dead is a failure branch, and is left out.
  - **Placing the markers.** `MWGui::QuestMarkers` tracks quests: one that starts or moves on is tracked, one that finishes isn't, and the newest quest is tracked when a game loads. It finds where each target is now, through the world model, and leaves out the dead, the disabled and the items the player already has.
  - **Doors.** `ESMStore` collects every door that leads elsewhere while it counts the content's objects at load. `MWWorld::findDoorRoute` finds the route through the fewest doors, so a target in another place is marked at the first door of the way there. On the world map, an indoor target is marked at its building's door.
  - **What the player sees.** The markers show on the 2D and 3D local maps, the HUD's minimap and the world map. A compass along the top of the HUD shows them too. A marker past the compass's ends stays at the nearer end, and an arrow says whether the target is above or below. The controller journal marks a tracked quest beside its name, and Back tracks or stops tracking the highlighted quest.
  - **Settings.** Four GUI settings: whether markers show, whether quests are tracked by themselves, which ways forward are marked (the next stage's only, or all of them), and the compass.
  - **Tests and limits.** Google Tests cover the analysis, the door routes and the compass's arithmetic. The `quest-markers` scenario checks tracking from the journal. The tracked quests aren't saved yet: they're rebuilt from the journal when a game loads.

  ingredients across a whole region. Second reviewers checked 279 stages and disagreed at 8. Engine changes that came
  out of it: a conversation's answers now get the conditions and speaker of the lines that offer the choice, which
  made six corrections unnecessary; targets can wait for several conditions at once, a global variable, or several of
  an item; and a quest's warning about a changed fingerprint gives the new one. With these, the last steps of the
  Hlaalu and Telvanni Hortator chapters could be marked, but they still need entries.
- The Imperial Cult, Imperial Legion and Tribunal Temple are reviewed, the third batch, and the three Great Houses,
  the fourth. Batch 3 has 255 stages: 138 right, 115 corrected, 2 unfixable. Batch 4 has 343: 167 right, 174
  corrected, 2 unfixable. Second reviewers disagreed at 16 and 13 stages. The last steps of the Hlaalu and Telvanni
  Hortator chapters are now marked, on the stage of each chapter's book quest. Engine changes from these batches:
  stages that a line sets without a journal entry are reviewed and can have entries, lines that need a faction rank
  the player lacks or gold the player doesn't carry are left out while playing, an answer in a conversation only
  counts at the stage its offering line sets, and the review sheets say when a placement is somewhere the markers
  can't reach.
