# Games to look at for gamepad UI

This is a reading list for deciding what the new UI should feel like. It is grouped by what each game is a good example of, not ranked. Each entry says why it is worth a look and what people criticized.

Web research on "best game UI" mostly returns opinion pieces. Entries backed by a review, developer talk or accessibility review cite that source. Entries marked *(general reputation)* rest on how the game is commonly discussed, and weren't checked against a source for this list.

## Morrowind's screens, for comparison

While browsing, it helps to look for these screens, because Morrowind has all of them:

- inventory and equipment, with a paper-doll character view,
- character sheet: attributes, skills, level progress, race, class, birthsign,
- spells and magic items,
- local map and world map,
- journal: quests, plus a topic index,
- dialogue, which in Morrowind is a list of clickable topics rather than a choice wheel,
- barter, training, travel and spell buying,
- crafting screens: alchemy, enchanting, spellmaking and repair,
- rest and wait,
- the HUD, and quick-select for weapons and spells.

## Closest relatives: open-world RPGs with deep menus

- **The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion Remastered (2025).** This is the closest match available. It is a Morrowind-era design with the same screens: spellmaking, alchemy, enchanting, repair and persuasion. The UI was rebuilt in 2025 for controllers. Critics said the UI was modernized and the menus easier to navigate ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25191767-elder-scrolls-iv-oblivion-remastered-review-gameplay-impressions-and-top-features), [IGN via AOL](https://www.aol.com/elder-scrolls-iv-oblivion-remastered-221145325.html)). Players complained that the inventory became cluttered and lost its sub-category filters ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2623190/discussions/0/604156952015028609)). Both what it got right and what it got wrong apply directly to us.
- **Skyrim (2011).** Older than ten years, but it's the obvious baseline: a vertical list of categories, then a list of items, with a 3D item preview. The PC mod SkyUI exists because the console-first list was slow with many items, and that criticism is worth understanding. The Starfield entry below covers the same story.
- **The Witcher 3 (2015, updated 2022).** *(general reputation)* A tabbed hub with inventory, map, quests, alchemy, crafting and character. A radial menu for quick selection. Item comparison tooltips. Often called dense but workable on a gamepad.
- **Kingdom Come: Deliverance II (2025).** A first-person medieval RPG with a paper-doll inventory. Its skeuomorphic style is close to Morrowind's parchment look. Reviews liked the style. They said the maps, inventory and quest log slide important details off the screen edge ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=456633)), and that the problem is the structure, not the drawing ([DTF breakdown](https://dtf.ru/3876402-razbor-interfeisa-kingdom-come-deliverance-ii)).
- **Avowed (2025).** A first-person fantasy RPG on Xbox and PC. PC Gamer called its very granular UI options, such as turning off markers, prompts and the tracker, one of its greatest strengths ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/my-favorite-thing-about-avowed-might-just-be-how-much-stuff-it-lets-me-turn-off)).
- **Elden Ring (2022).** *(general reputation)* Controller-native, with a grid inventory, a minimal HUD and quick-swap slots on the d-pad. Criticized for terse, hard-to-parse item descriptions.
- **Baldur's Gate 3 on console (2023).** Larian replaced the PC hotbar with radial menus for controller ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/09/05/how-baldurs-gate-3-adapts-its-expansive-rpg-gameplay-for-your-dualsense-controller/)). Some players found the result cleaner than the mouse UI. Others disliked the radials and the merged character sheet and inventory ([RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/feature/baldurs-gate-iii-on-console-is-good-enough/)). This is the best modern example of a heavy CRPG adapted to a gamepad.

## Readability and accessibility

- **God of War (2018) and God of War Ragnarök (2022).** Text is readable at default size and scales up to very large sizes, consistently across menus. There are vision, hearing and motor presets, and separate sizes for UI text, subtitles and icons ([Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/?p=10258), [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/05/19/god-of-war-ragnarok-accessibility-features-revealed/)). Gear menus use the bumpers for tabs and show clear stat comparisons. One critique says Ragnarök's menus got denser than the 2018 game's.
- **Horizon Zero Dawn (2017) and Horizon Forbidden West (2022).** *(general reputation)* A weapon wheel, a categorized inventory, and a clean, legible sci-fi style. Forbidden West added inventory sorting categories, but players still found item management heavy.

## Strong visual identity

- **Persona 5 Royal (2016/2019) and Metaphor: ReFantazio (2024).** These are widely cited as the most stylish game menus. Each screen is designed and built on its own. The director said Persona 5's menus were "impossible to read at first" and needed a lot of tuning to become legible ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/jrpg/persona-director-and-jrpg-veteran-says-the-amazing-menus-in-metaphor-refantazio-were-really-annoying-to-make-just-as-persona-5s-were-impossible-to-read-at-first), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/metaphor-refantazio-persona-5-menu-ui-hashino-1851667088)). Worth looking at for how far style can go, and what it costs to build.
- **Ghost of Tsushima (2020).** A minimal HUD. The wind points toward your objective instead of a waypoint marker. The developers said they "tried very hard to not have UI because it hurts immersion" ([Keengamer, quoting GameSpot](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/news/prepare-yourself-as-ghost-of-tsushima-is-going-to-be-hard/)). Some reviewers found the menus clunky under pressure.
- **Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018).** *(general reputation)* A weapon wheel and menus styled as period catalogs. Very immersive, and often criticized as slow.

## Simple and fast

- **Breath of the Wild (2017) and Tears of the Kingdom (2023).** Inventory is horizontal rows of items, one per category. The bumpers switch category, and holding a d-pad direction quick-selects. People praise it as clean and utilitarian. Tears of the Kingdom shows where the design breaks down: with many more items and crafting, players had to open, close and reopen menus to find things ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/tears-of-the-kingdom-how-nintendo-improved-and-ignored-ui-issues-843f094b14b2)).
- **Hades (2020).** *(general reputation)* Few screens, large readable text, and quick decisions. A good example of controller UI that never feels like a port.
- **Diablo III on console (2013) and Diablo IV (2023).** Diablo III's console version replaced the PC grid with tabs and radial menus. A Game Developer blogger called it the best tab and radial menu system any console action RPG had shipped ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/diablo-iii-sets-the-standard-for-console-ports)). Diablo III is older than ten years, but it is a well-known example of redesigning a PC inventory for a pad rather than porting it.

## Cursor-based controller UI

- **Destiny (2014) and Destiny 2 (2017).** Bungie gave controller menus a free cursor instead of stepping between boxes. Icons grow under the cursor, and the cursor slows over buttons using the same code as aim assist. The GDC 2016 talk "Tenacious Design and The Interface of 'Destiny'" covers why ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023107/Tenacious-Design-and-The-Interface), [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/destiny-2-menu)). This is the main alternative to d-pad focus navigation, and OpenMW already has a gamepad cursor mode.

## Cautionary examples

- **Starfield (2023).** Bethesda's most recent game. IGN called its inventory management "shockingly bad", and a mod, StarUI, rebuilt the inventory into compact sortable columns within a week of launch ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/rejoice-theres-already-a-starfield-interface-overhaul-called-starui), [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/starfield-ui-mod-provides-much-needed-improvements-to-inventory-system)). It shows the usual failure of console-first lists when there are hundreds of items.
- **Disco Elysium: The Final Cut on console (2021).** The game is text-heavy and dialogue-driven, which makes it the closest match to Morrowind's dialogue. Its console version was criticized for awkward controller mapping, hard-to-read dialogue text, and confusing choice selection ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/disco-elysium-final-cut), [Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/2021/03/31/disco-elysium-the-final-cut-can-i-play-that-accessibility-review/)). It shows what to avoid when putting lots of text on a TV.

## Where to browse

- **[Game UI Database](https://www.gameuidatabase.com/)** has over 55,000 screenshots from more than 1,300 games, filterable by screen type, such as inventory, map, dialogue or shop ([Game Developer on its relaunch](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-ui-database-relaunches-with-new-features-video-support-and-over-55-000-screenshots)). It is the fastest way to compare, for example, ten different inventory screens side by side. Most of the games above are in it.
- **[Interface In Game](https://interfaceingame.com/)** has screenshots and videos sorted by game and by element type: Inventory, Character, Stats, Dialogue, Map, Quest, Store and Skill tree.
- **YouTube:** searching "<game> menus walkthrough" or "<game> UI" usually finds a video of someone paging through every screen. That shows motion and navigation, which still images miss.
